AI + CREDIT DOCUMENTS

Translating Complex U.S. Credit Agreements into Structured Financial Data

Served as a subject-matter bridge between client requirements and internal Operations, QA, and Engineering teams, translating complex Private Credit and Broadly Syndicated Loan provisions into structured extraction logic.

Credit AgreementsCovenantsTaxonomyBusiness Rules

The challenge

Credit agreements often distribute the meaning of a provision across definitions, provisos, exceptions, cross-references, and multiple covenant sections. The challenge was to determine how that language should be interpreted and represented as structured data.

My role

Participated in 100+ hours of client knowledge-transfer sessions and acted as the primary bridge between the client and internal Operations, QA, and Engineering teams. I translated requirements into definitions, prompt rules, extraction logic, and evaluation criteria.

What I worked on

  • Translated Private Credit and BSL concepts into structured extraction requirements.
  • Created definitions, prompt rules, business logic, and evaluation criteria.
  • Worked with module owners to resolve development questions and implementation edge cases.
  • Supported researchers, created evaluation sheets with automated data checks and sanity checks, and audited research outputs.
  • Helped group related datapoints into efficient research workflows and supported resource planning.
  • Worked directly with the client SME to surface ambiguous cases and confirm appropriate treatment.

Examples of complex analysis

Ratio Calculation & Testing

Reviewed testing frequency, borrower election rights, applicable baskets, exclusions, and whether narrowly drafted language operated at the covenant level.

Debt Definition & Exclusions

Traced inclusions, embedded parenthetical exclusions, and separate exclusions to determine the final treatment of debt.

J.Crew / Trapdoor

Evaluated structural mechanics that could be embedded within broader subsidiary or investment provisions rather than appearing under an obvious heading.

Basket Sharing & Reallocation

Traced how capacity could be shared or reallocated across covenant frameworks while avoiding double counting.

Core capability: translating ambiguous contractual language into consistent, explainable structured-data rules while keeping client, research, QA, and Engineering teams aligned.
02

DATA QUALITY + AI

AI Extraction Validation & Financial Data Quality

Investigated model reasoning and source context to separate research issues from model interpretation errors, then converted recurring failures into clearer definitions, prompt rules, examples, and validation controls.

AI QARCAPrompt RulesValidationHuman-in-the-loop
Read case study

Common failure patterns

Missing exceptions, treating carve-outs as inclusions, focusing on non-anchor language while overlooking key anchors, and failing to follow defined terms when they contained the required information.

Root-cause workflow

Investigated the model's reasoning, returned to the underlying step files and source context, identified where the reasoning diverged from the extraction requirement, then updated the definition or prompt and reran the extraction.

Example — Asset Sale Sweep: Baskets Covered

The model could identify an apparent inclusion in the main clause and stop, even when a defined term elsewhere contained additional required inclusions. In other cases, it returned only one basket when multiple selections were required.

I traced the reasoning back to the source context, identified the incorrect stopping logic, added explicit rules for following defined terms and capturing all applicable selections, provided one-shot examples where useful, and reran the extraction to verify the correction.

Data-quality controls

Field validation

Structured fields accepted only appropriate value types, reducing invalid manual inputs such as text entered into percentage or dropdown fields.

Reconciliation

Manual research was reconciled against a consolidated master dataset used by Engineering for output comparison and accuracy review.

Sanity checks

Validation conditions could prevent data transfer when required checks were not satisfied, catching issues before they progressed downstream.

Core capability: turning individual AI extraction failures into reusable rules, evaluation criteria, and quality controls rather than treating each error as an isolated exception.

PROCESS AUTOMATION

Financial Data Quality Automation — Fund Name Validator

Built and deployed a lightweight validation application to address recurring fund-name extraction issues in Schedule of Investments workflows, reducing manual matching time from about 15 minutes to under 5 minutes per document.

PythonStreamlitGitHubData MatchingExcel
Read case study

The problem

QA identified header rows, incomplete fund names, and other inconsistencies in extracted fund-name results. Long SOI lists made manual matching slow and difficult.

The solution

Built a Fund Name Validator that compares extracted fund names against a client-provided master list and generates an Excel output for quick review.

How the tool worked

  1. User uploads the client master list and the application output file.
  2. The validator compares extracted fund names with the master list.
  3. Exact matches, partial matches, and no-match results are clearly identified.
  4. The corresponding master fund name is included for side-by-side comparison.
  5. The user downloads the generated Excel file for QA review.

Deployment & adoption

I built and deployed the tool using Python/Streamlit, maintained the code through GitHub, and hosted the application on Streamlit Cloud so users did not need a local technical setup. The QA team tested the tool and it moved into regular operational use.
