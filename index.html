Generalized to protect client confidentiality. No client names, internal fields or proprietary prompts are shared.

Turning complex credit agreements into structured extraction rules

Result: 100+ hours of client knowledge transfer became the definitions, prompt rules and evaluation criteria that Operations, QA and Engineering built on.

Read the case study

Credit agreements spread one provision across definitions, provisos, exceptions and cross-references. The job was to decide how that language should be read and represented as data, for both Private Credit and broadly syndicated loans.

I was the main bridge between the client and our Operations, QA and Engineering teams. I wrote definitions, prompt rules and business logic, built evaluation sheets with automated sanity checks, audited research outputs, and took ambiguous cases to the client expert for a ruling.

  • Ratio testing. Testing frequency, borrower election rights, applicable baskets and exclusions.
  • Debt definition. Tracing inclusions, embedded exclusions and separate carve-outs to a final treatment.
  • Trapdoor structures. Mechanics buried in subsidiary or investment provisions, not under an obvious heading.
  • Basket sharing. How capacity is shared or reallocated across covenants without double counting.

Finding why an AI model got a covenant wrong, then fixing the rule

Result: Individual extraction failures became reusable rules, examples and validation controls instead of one-off corrections.

Read the case study

Typical failures: missing an exception, treating a carve-out as an inclusion, stopping at the main clause when a defined term held more of the answer.

Example. On a mandatory-prepayment field that needed every applicable basket, the model found one inclusion in the main clause and stopped, or returned a single basket when several applied. I traced its reasoning back to the source text, found the faulty stopping logic, added explicit rules for following defined terms and capturing all selections, gave one-shot examples, and reran the extraction to confirm the fix.

Controls added. Field validation so percentage and dropdown fields only accepted valid values. Reconciliation of manual research against a consolidated master dataset. Sanity checks that blocked data transfer when required conditions failed.

Fund Name Validator: a small tool that removed a daily bottleneck

Result: Manual fund-name matching dropped from about 15 minutes to under 5 per document. QA adopted it for regular use.

Read the case study

QA kept finding header rows, incomplete fund names and other errors in extracted fund names, and long Schedule of Investments lists made manual matching slow.

I built a Python and Streamlit app. QA uploads the client master list and the extraction output; the app flags exact, partial and no-match results, shows the matching master name alongside, and exports an Excel file for review. It was deployed so QA needed no local setup, and the team used it on sanitized data only. Code is maintained in GitHub.
